The Dancing Plague of 1518
Video Title: In 1518, Hundreds Danced Until They Dropped 💃😨
Script (about 130 words, 52 seconds)
[Hook, 0-5s]
In 1518, hundreds of people couldn't stop dancing. Some danced until they died.
[Main, 5-40s]
July 1518. Strasbourg, in what is now France.
A woman named Frau Troffea stepped into the street and started dancing. No music. No reason. She kept going for days.
Within a week, dozens had joined her. Within a month, around 400 people were dancing. Records say they weren't having fun. They cried. They begged for help.
Doctors had a diagnosis: "hot blood."
[Twist, 40-50s]
So what was the cure? More dancing.
Officials hired musicians, opened guildhalls, and even built a stage. They thought the dancers needed to dance it out. It made things worse. Some reportedly collapsed from exhaustion, heart attacks, or strokes.
[Ending, 50-60s]
Historians still argue about the cause. The leading theory is mass psychogenic illness, triggered by extreme stress and famine. Could fear make a whole city lose control? Tell me below.

- Music: Start with an eerie, off-kilter medieval folk loop (fiddle, drum) that gets faster and more distorted as the crowd grows. Cut to silence for one beat before the twist.

Visual Suggestions (9:16)
Medieval woodcut or engraving style animation (Hendrik Hondius's 1642 engraving of dancing sufferers is a good reference, and is public domain), a simple map pin on Strasbourg, silhouettes of a growing crowd, and a wooden stage graphic for the twist.
Fact-Check Note
The exact number of dancers and deaths is debated. Some sources claim around 15 deaths per day, but historians consider this unreliable, so the script says "reportedly" and "around 400."
